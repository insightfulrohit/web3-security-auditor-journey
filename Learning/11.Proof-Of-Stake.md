# Proof of Stake (PoS)

In blockchain technology, validators get rewards when they successfully add a new block to the network.

Unlike Proof of Work (PoW), there is no need to solve complex cryptographic puzzles. Instead, validators are chosen based on the amount of cryptocurrency they hold and are willing to "stake" in the network.

---

## How the Selection Works

In Proof of Stake, validators lock (stake) their cryptocurrency as collateral.

The network then selects a validator to create the next block based on factors such as:

- Amount of stake
- Duration of staking
- Randomization (in some systems)

---

## Block Creation Process

1. Validator stakes coins in the network
2. Network selects a validator
3. Selected validator creates and proposes a new block
4. Other validators verify the block
5. If valid, the block is added to the blockchain

---

## Rewards and Penalties

- The selected validator receives a reward for adding the block
- Some systems also give transaction fees as rewards

If a validator behaves maliciously:

- A portion of their stake can be **slashed** (penalty)
- They may be removed from the network

---

## What About Other Validators?

- Other validators do not receive block rewards
- They continue participating in validation for future chances

---

## Why It is Called Proof of Stake

Because validators prove their participation and honesty by staking their own cryptocurrency, this mechanism is called **Proof of Stake**.

---

## Key Differences from Proof of Work

- No heavy computation required
- Energy-efficient
- Faster block creation
- Security depends on economic stake instead of computational power

---

## Summary

Proof of Stake is a consensus mechanism where validators are selected based on their stake in the network. It reduces energy consumption and improves efficiency while maintaining security through economic incentives and penalties.
